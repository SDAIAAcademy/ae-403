Burn rate

Burn rate is how fast the budget is being spent

1×
spends the 30-day budget in 30 days
6×
spends 5% of it in 6 hours
14.4×
spends 2% of it in one hour

Burn rate = error rate in the window ÷ (1 − objective). With a 95% quality objective, 14.4× means 72% of answers failing.

The SRE workbook's recommended starting points: page at 14.4× over one hour and five minutes, and at 6× over six hours and thirty minutes; ticket at 1× over three days. They were designed for availability budgets of 0.1%, where 14.4× is an error rate of 1.4%.

Sources: Google SRE workbook, Alerting on SLOs.

Multi-window alerts

Two windows: one proves it matters, one proves it is still happening

def fires(g, long, short, rate):
    lw = U.burn_rate(g.bad, g.graded, 0.95, long)
    sw = U.burn_rate(g.bad, g.graded, 0.95, short)
    return (lw > rate) & (sw > rate)

Without the short window, the alert keeps firing for hours after the fix; without the long one, a single bad five minutes pages someone.

Windows are counted in five-minute slots: one hour is 12 slots. Ask: what happens to a regression that affects ten minutes in every hour? The workbook discusses this weakness of short windows.

Incident roles

Three roles keep deciding, doing and recording apart

Incident lead

Decides and sets priorities. Talks to the service owner. Does not type commands.

Operator

Runs the mitigation and reports what the system shows.

Scribe

Keeps the decision log: minute, role, decision, reason.

The SRE workbook's incident response chapter separates command, operations and communications roles. Three is enough for a team of Athar's size; the separation matters more than the titles.

Sources: Google SRE workbook, Incident Response.

SDAIA's procedural guide for handling personal data breach incidents: notify SDAIA within 72 hours of becoming aware when the incident is expected to harm the data or the data subjects, through the national platform; contain; notify data subjects without undue delay when it harms them; keep the documents. What goes wrong

The 72 hours start at awareness, not at the root cause

  • Awareness came at T+9, when a customer reported another person's address
  • Waiting for the root cause before assessing spends the clock on the wrong question
  • Preserve the evidence before the rollback overwrites the logs you will need

The breach guide lists what the notice contains: a description, the categories and numbers of people, the risks and remedies, whether data subjects are told, and contacts. The assessment at T+20 gathers exactly these.

Lab 3.3 · the old pipeline

Ten minutes at 5% promoted the int4 model on 27 Arabic answers

Latency

Canary p95 1,174 ms against stable 1,455 ms: the 4-bit model is faster.

Quality

26 of 27 graded Arabic answers passed. Its true Arabic rate is 90.5%, against 97% for stable.

The true rates are the simulator's settings; the participants see only the samples. With 27 samples a 90% model passes the gate by luck about a quarter of the time; on this run it did.

The minimum sample

No quality verdict below 200 graded answers per language

  • Latency is judged at every step: it has thousands of samples
  • Quality is judged on the cumulative canary samples, once each language has 200
  • Before that the step continues, and the release cannot reach 100%
  • Canary answers are graded at 30%, stable ones at 5%, to reach 200 in minutes

200 is a course choice: it detects a drop of a few points from 97% with reasonable confidence. Participants must justify their own number in the capstone. The SRE workbook's canarying chapter discusses population size and the reduced overall error rate a canary sees.

Sources: Google SRE workbook, Canarying Releases.

Blameless reviews

A blameless review asks what made the error easy, not who made it

Not a factor

"The engineer did not test Arabic."

A factor

"The evaluation set had no Arabic tracking questions and no Eastern Arabic digits."

The SRE book: a blameless postmortem focuses on the contributing causes without indicting any individual or team.

The second sentence leads to a fix any engineer benefits from; the first leads to a more careful engineer and the same gap. Lab 3.4's check flags names and phrases such as "human error" and "should have".

Sources: Google SRE book, Postmortem Culture.

Lab 3.4 · INC-0731

Five conditions made the incident possible

  1. The offline evaluation set had no Arabic tracking questions
  2. The v2.4 prompt template dropped digit normalisation
  3. The tracking lookup fell back to the nearest parcel instead of refusing
  4. The release went to everyone at once, with no quality gate
  5. On-call had no written authority to roll back

Each factor in the lab carries its evidence from the record (a chat line, a fact, a timeline entry). Factor 3 is why a quality bug became a personal data breach: an unsafe default in a dependency.

From case to test

The incident's cases become a test that failed before the fix

def check_case(answer, case):
    a = U.normalise_digits(answer)
    problems = []
    if case["asked_parcel"] not in a:
        problems.append("does not name the parcel asked about")
    if [p for p in U.PARCEL_RE.findall(a) if p != case["asked_parcel"]]:
        problems.append("names another customer's parcel")
    return problems

v2.4 passes 0 of 12 cases; v2.4.1 passes 12 of 12.

Replaying recorded failures as regression cases was AE-305's Day 4 technique; here the source is the review. A test that passes on the broken version proves nothing: make it fail first.
