The exit check mirrors the entry check: an error budget burn, an admission limit from a goodput curve, and the first actions of an incident in order. It is 20% of the grade and runs with AI off; collect the papers before the first defence.

The capstone

A readiness review of a real or supplied service, with measured evidence

  • A public GitHub repository: the review, the code and notebooks behind every number, the logs
  • Load-test, chaos and pipeline logs, the drill's decision log, and a commit history that shows the work
  • Findings ranked by risk, a launch decision, and dated conditions that each name their proof
  • Synthetic data only: no personal data, no keys, no private model weights

How reviews are scored

The rubric

Objectives and alerts
20%
Capacity and degradation
25%
Incident drill and review
20%
Release integrity
15%
Readiness decision
20%
Every claim carries a number and the log it came from. "Met" is the team's word; a review shows the measurement.

Full marks, one example each. Objectives: per-language quality with its sample size and interval, and a burn-rate alert replayed on data. Capacity: a goodput curve, the busiest hour and the margin. Degradation: chaos results per mode with quality measured in Arabic and English. Drill: a decision log with times. Integrity: a gate that rejects a tampered and an unlicensed artefact, with logs. Decision: ranked findings and dated conditions with proofs.

The grade: rubric 60%, panel defence 20%, supervised check 20%. Passing needs 70% overall and at least 60% on both the rubric and the supervised check.

Format

Ten minutes of review, then five of questions with a live fault

1 · The service

What it does, for whom, its objectives. Two minutes.

2 · The evidence

Capacity, degradation, the drill, the release gate: measured. Five minutes.

3 · The decision

The top three findings, the decision, the conditions. Three minutes.

Peer feedback. After each defence, everyone writes one strength, one question and one suggestion.

Ten and five minutes is the course's choice for an Expert-level defence with a live diagnosis; the catalog entry does not fix it. Keep the timer visible and stop the talk at ten: a review that cannot be told in ten minutes has not been ranked.

This is the authenticity check of the catalog entry. On the supplied service, inject one of the simulator's faults in a notebook the participant can see: s = relia.Service(); s.faults.add("retrieval_outage"), or "retrieval_slow", or "provider_timeout"; or replay overload with retries through relia.retry_storm. On a peer's service, use Toxiproxy for latency or a cut connection, agreed with the owner beforehand. Choose the fault the participant's own review rated lowest: it tests whether the ranking was right.

What the panel will look for

The week in four questions

What does good mean?

Indicators per language, budgets that change releases.

What survives the busiest hour?

An operating point with margin, shedding by priority, honest degraded modes.

Who acts when it breaks?

Roles, a decision log, a canary that rolls back, a review without blame.

What are we running?

Hashes, signatures, a bill of materials, a ranked launch decision.

One question per day. Each participant should be able to point to where their repository answers each one.
