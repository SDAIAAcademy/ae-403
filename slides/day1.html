Four new failure modes

An AI service can be up and still fail its users

Wrong

Fluent answers that are false, often in one language only.

Slow to start

Long prompts wait in the queue; the first token takes seconds.

Slow to stream

The answer arrives word by word, slower than reading speed.

Empty or cut

A 200 with nothing useful: the output limit cut the answer.

All four return HTTP 200. A status-code dashboard counts them as success.

Ask the room for an example of each from their own services. Then: which of the four does your current alerting see? Usually none.

Why the uptime check is green

A probe measures the probe, not the users

The probeA real Athar request
one tiny English prompt a minutea question plus 1,000 to 3,000 tokens of retrieved articles
needs no retrievalfails when the index is wrong or down
four output tokens60 to 300 tokens, streamed
never gradedright or wrong, in Arabic or English

The probe is still useful: it is the only thing that sees a total outage at night, when there are no users to complain. Keep it; never count it as a user (Lab 1.2).

What goes wrong in the lab

At night, small numbers look like incidents

  • Two failed answers out of eight graded is a 75% pass rate
  • One empty answer out of ten requests is 10%
  • Require a minimum sample (40 graded answers in 30 minutes) and a minimum duration (15 minutes)

The rule behind it

An indicator without a sample size is an opinion. Every quality number this week comes with its count or its interval.

Participants who skip the minimum sample find twenty or more one-minute incidents, almost all at night. Let them, then ask what an on-call engineer would do with twenty pages a week.

Latency is two numbers

First-token time and per-token time fail for different reasons

Time to first token

Queueing plus prefill of the whole prompt. Grows with load and with prompt length. Users feel it as "is it working?"

Time per output token

One decode step of the batch. Grows with the batch size. Users feel it as reading slower than the text appears.

Athar's thresholds: first token within 1,500 ms, then within 100 ms a token. On the lab day 94.2% and 98.9% of served requests met them.

An end-to-end latency hides both: a long answer that streams well looks slow, a short answer stuck in the queue looks fast. Day 2 shows which knob moves which number.

Graders follow a written rubric; the sample is random, not the answers someone complained about.

Lab 1.2 numbers. The Arabic interval's upper end (96.7%) is below the English rate (98.0%): the gap is not noise. Sundus Al-Zubaidi's team grades about 6% of answers; with a model grader, the grader itself needs calibration against people (AE-305 Day 3).

Responsible use

Every quality objective is sliced by language

  • Athar's traffic is 60% Arabic: an average lets English mask an Arabic failure
  • Degraded modes and model updates fail one language more often than the other
  • The slice is part of the objective, not a dashboard filter

The question for any review

"Show me this number for Arabic users only." If the team cannot, the objective is not defined yet.

This is the entry's responsible-use requirement: a degraded mode must not fail one language unnoticed. It returns on Day 2 (fallback quality by language) and Day 3 (the alert that only fires per language).

Write it down

An indicator specification fits on five lines

availability: good = status 200; valid = users (not probe), not 499
first_token:  good = ttft_ms <= 1500 among served user requests
per_token:    good = tpot_ms <= 100 among served user requests
quality_ar:   good = passed; valid = graded Arabic answers (random 6%)
quality_en:   good = passed; valid = graded English answers (random 6%)

Have participants keep this text: it is the first section of the capstone's objectives document. A reviewer will challenge each "valid" clause.

Objectives and error budgets

  • Set an objective from a user task, not from last month

03
An objective

Set the objective from the user's task, then check it against history

From the task

A customer waiting at the door needs the parcel status before the courier leaves: a first token within 1.5 s, an answer that is right.

Not from last month

"We had 99.82%, so the objective is 99.8%" makes every bad month a target and every good one a waste.

Athar's four: availability 99.5%; first token within 1,500 ms for 95%; quality 95% for Arabic and 95% for English; all over a rolling 30 days.

Ruwaida Al-Sharari owns the objectives; you propose them and defend them. History is a sanity check: an objective far above what the service has ever achieved is a wish.

Budget arithmetic

The error budget is the bad events the objective allows

Burned = bad events / (1 - target) / valid events. It is a count of users, not of minutes.

What goes wrong

An objective just below normal leaves no room to work

  • Arabic passes 97.2% on a normal day; the objective is 95%
  • So 56% of the budget goes on days when nothing is wrong
  • Either improve normal Arabic quality, or accept that every Arabic incident freezes releases
  • Both are decisions for the service owner, made with the numbers, before the incident

This is the reviewer's challenge participants must be ready for in the capstone: "Why 95%?" A good answer names the user task and the cost of the budget it leaves.

A release policy

The policy decides from the remaining budget of what the change touches

Lowest remaining budget among the touched objectivesDecision
50% or morego
25% to 50%go behind a canary
below 25%freeze: reliability and security fixes only

The SRE workbook's example policy halts changes other than urgent and security fixes once a service exceeds its budget over four weeks.

Our thresholds are a course choice, stricter than the workbook example (which freezes at an exhausted budget). Decide them with the service owner and write them down before you need them.

Sources: Google SRE workbook, Example Error Budget Policy.

The flow: identify the parcel, check eligibility, offer slots, confirm. A request-level dashboard is green on every one of these calls. The user's task is the booking.

Indicators for a flow

Three indicators describe a flow: completion, cost and escalation

Completion

Sessions that reached the goal, per language. 78.2% Arabic, 81.3% English.

Cost per completed task

All tokens, including failed sessions, over completed ones: SAR 0.0133 a booking.

Escalation

Handed to a person: 8.1% Arabic, 7.2% English. A signal, not a failure.

The SAR rate is a synthetic internal cost of the self-hosted engine (SAR 0.002 per 1,000 tokens), so the numbers are comparable, not a price.

Lab 1.4 · the loops

customerNot that slot.

64 of 2,000 sessions made more than ten calls; they completed 30% of the time. A step budget or a "show the whole day" fallback is the fix; the indicator is what makes the loop visible.

Escalation

Escalation is the right answer for some parcels; the hand-over is the objective

  • A parcel held by customs cannot be rebooked online: escalating it is correct
  • Pushing escalation to zero makes the assistant book what it should not
  • Measure the hand-over: every escalated session reaches a person, in its language

Ask the room for their own flow's equivalent: a refund above a limit, a medical question, a legal one. The objective is on the quality of the hand-over, not the count.
